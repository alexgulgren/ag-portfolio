Bridging the gap between hardware and software

Connecting a generator to the Kohler Energy Management app was one of the top three reasons people called support. I redesigned the setup flow so the app finds the generator on its own, asks for one thing at a time, and guides people through the rest without sending them anywhere else.

Context

The problem

Rehlko's Home Energy apps let dealers, technicians and homeowners monitor and control generators and automatic transfer switches. Before any of that works, the generator has to be connected to the app, and that's where many people got stuck.

  • A hard-to-find PIN. Locating the controller PIN took technical, complicated steps.
  • Manual entry. People typed in serial numbers by hand, even though the device could connect using their login.
  • Leaving the app. When setup went wrong, people were sent to external resources, which added confusion instead of solving it.

The result was that generator connection became one of the top three issues driving support requests.

Research

Understanding the journey

  1. Discovery. I mapped use cases and the end-to-end journey, found the pain and friction points, and gathered input from stakeholders.
  2. Hands-on testing. I tested the firmware myself and reviewed customer support's troubleshooting documentation to see where people really got stuck.
  3. Ideation. I brainstormed and sketched quickly, then pitched early concepts with the pros and cons of each to set a direction.
  4. Validation. I ran structured evaluations and moderated user tests, iterating on the flow between rounds.

Solution

What we shipped

Automatic generator detection. The app finds the generator, so people skip typing the serial number.

One thing per screen. I split the serial number and PIN into separate steps to lower cognitive load.

An in-app setup guide. Visual, step-by-step instructions keep people in the app when they need help, instead of sending them to outside documentation.

Collaboration

Who made it work

Hardware onboarding crosses teams, so I brought them in early:

  • Customer service set up tracking for setup clicks and PIN-related support calls.
  • Mechanical engineers provided CAD renderings so the in-app guide shows the real hardware.
  • Product marketing updated the user guide to match the new flow.
