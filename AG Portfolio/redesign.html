GoReact · Platform redesign · Lead UX Designer

Redesigning a platform so it could grow into new markets

GoReact's navigation was fragmented, its design system was outdated, and its language was built for higher ed. Instead of patching issues one at a time, I proposed and led a system-level redesign. Every user adopted it, and satisfaction rose by about 30%.

Role

Lead UX Designer, design system owner

Team

1 PM, 6 engineers, 1 QA

Markets

Higher ed, professional development, corporate training, healthcare

Tools

Figma, Zeroheight, UsabilityHub

Outcomes

What changed

100%of users adopted the redesign
~30%higher satisfaction score
4new initiatives it made possible, including the AI Assistant and free trial

Context

The problem

  • Fragmented information architecture made core workflows inconsistent and blocked new features.
  • Inconsistent modules increased cognitive load and wore down trust in the product.
  • A design system with little documentation and no governance made consistency hard and onboarding slow.

"It's hard to find my content and my learners' content because of the limited search options and poor navigation."

Instructor

"If we want to enter new verticals and scale, we need to remove the higher ed constraints that don't work for other markets."

Internal stakeholder

Learners said courses, activities and instructions were hard to find. Some instructors had written their own documentation just to use the product.

Research

Evidence

I combined customer interviews, heat map analysis, preference testing, guerrilla testing and a pilot.

  • Heat maps showed rage clicks in navigation, a clear sign of confusion.
  • Users had built workarounds for information architecture problems.
  • List view preferences varied by content type, so people needed flexible layouts.
  • Preference testing was nearly unanimous in favor of the proposed direction.

Success meant lower cognitive load and task time in key flows, fewer rage clicks, and more market and feature opportunities.

Solution

What we shipped

Dashboard. Breadcrumb navigation that makes content spaces clear, search at every level, robust sorting and filtering, a grid or list view, and a side panel for tools. I also changed the vocabulary to work across markets: "students" became "learners" and "courses" became "folders."

Playback and feedback panel. A restructured secondary navigation, simpler panel navigation that leaves room for new features, persistent tools, rich text editing and modern components, all with a better mobile experience.

Design system. I led the move from Adobe XD to Figma, built reusable components, consolidated icons into one library, and documented color usage and accessibility standards.

Impact

Why it mattered

The redesign reduced time to complete core tasks and started conversations about expanding into new markets. It also laid the foundation for the work that followed: a self-directed free trial, the AI Assistant, a student practice center and a skills-based marketplace.
